# Grux OS 3.0: always armed, built for the Mac entrepreneur, noise out

Status: approved 2026-09-20. This is the design the 3.0 plans argue from.

## The one sentence

Grux listens all the time, decides in under a second whether you were talking to
it, and does the reversible thing on the spot; everything a developer could do
in 1.x is still there, behind a door, and nothing the entrepreneur did not ask
for is on screen.

## Who it is for

The person running a business from a Mac. They may write code; most do not.
Every capability from 1.x survives. What changes is the surface: what is a
sidebar row, what is a door, what needs a key, and how the app talks.

## What 3.0 changes

### 1. Listening

- One control, three states: **Always on** (default), **After "Hey Grux"**,
  **Off**. Always on replaces the separate wake word, ambient and auto-send
  switches. The wake word stays as the opt-in downgrade.
- Every transcript chunk is judged: command, or not. When a TypeSafe key is
  present the judgment is made by Jev (a typed-decision model, roughly half a
  second, confidence attached). Without a key the judgment is made on device by
  the existing keyword and trigger matching. Adding the key changes nothing
  else; every decision point upgrades at once.
- Audio never leaves the machine. Only transcript text reaches the decision
  provider, and only when a key is configured.
- Hands-free scope is a fixed policy, not a setting:
  - **On the spot** (reversible): navigate Grux, open, close or focus apps and
    windows, start or stop a meeting note, dictate into chat and send, run a
    saved macro with no shell step, timers and reminders, music, read aloud,
    create a note or task, mute, search, screenshot into chat.
  - **Asks first** (stops in Approvals): send an email or message, reply on
    someone's behalf, delete or move a file, anything touching payments,
    accounts, transfers, checkout, or a privacy-costing permission. Permission
    prompts are shown when the action needs them, never at random.
  - **Never by voice alone**: run a shell command, publish or post publicly,
    payments, grant a macOS permission.
- The execute threshold defaults to 0.70. Above it the command runs; below it
  Grux asks with one tap; chatter is ignored. The threshold lives in Tuning.
- A destructive action never executes on the decision model alone. DecisionGate
  and Approvals stay in the path and a test proves a 0.99-confidence `rm -rf`
  still stops.

### 2. Where the decision shows

- The Ambient HUD carries the live stream on demand: chatter in gray, decisions
  in green with their latency.
- A live rail inside Chat shows the same stream.
- The menu bar item shows the last decision and its latency.
- A macOS notification banner per executed decision. The first time on a
  device, one explanatory banner precedes it.
- The orb reads ARMED, MUTED or SPEAKING, and the same state shows on the menu
  bar, the floating orb and the HUD.
- Cost and latency: on orb hover, in a Settings Usage card, and as one line in
  the morning briefing. Nothing permanent in the face.

### 3. The rail

Twelve rows, then two doors, then Settings:

Home, Chat, Mail, Calendar, Notes, Documents, Contacts, Tasks, Meetings,
Schedules, Integrations, Studio; **Developer** (collapsed, counted); **Labs**
(collapsed, counted); Settings.

Every registry row has exactly one door:

| Disposition | Surfaces |
|---|---|
| Rail | Home, Chat, Mail (was Mailbox, count is what needs you), Calendar, Notes, Documents, Contacts, Tasks, Meetings, Schedules, Integrations, Studio, Settings |
| Folds into a parent | Speakers into Meetings; Workflows into Schedules; Outbound Webhooks into Integrations; Compose and send into Mail (own credential); Projects into Tasks (the grouping it already is); Folders into Settings as the files allowlist; Skills into Chat as a composer picker; Approvals into a global tray with a badge; Focus log into a Today card |
| Developer door | Commands, Terminal Focus, Agents, Local Models (the picker also appears in onboarding), Compare |
| Labs door | The whole app-about-itself cluster, all seven: Reactor, Jax HQ, Jax Command, Cognition Map, Feature Review, Self-Upgrade (also `grux upgrade`), Roadmap; plus Phone companion. Decided as a cluster on 2026-09-20 and it overrides the per-row answers for Cognition Map, Feature Review and Self-Upgrade |
| Brand-scoped | Meta Ads, Social, Outreach: rows appear only once a brand exists; onboarding names "Add a brand" |
| Ripped | Domain monitor |

Chrome that stays: the orb and state pill, the focus card, the command palette
(named at first run), the menu bar item. Orb Anywhere (the floating desktop
orb with its mic-muted button) is ripped on 2026-09-20: it was noise beside the
focus card, which is the one floating surface that stays.

The Developer door opens from the onboarding tier ("I write code") or a
Settings switch. Labs is badged once at the door; per-row BETA pills go.

### 4. Home becomes Today

Your name, then three cards (Next, Mail that needs you, Watching), Start my
day, one line inviting you to just say it, then the briefing. Chat stays the
landing tab.

### 5. Decision points that route through the decision engine

Ambient command versus chatter with slots; window and app target resolution;
intent routing in Chat; shell destructive check as a second opinion; the email
triage classify step; DecisionGate; notification classification; focus drift;
the mail needs-you score; the approvals risk score; task priority; meeting
moment detection. Text generation (summaries, memories, replies) and speaker
attribution stay where they are.

### 6. The finish list

Every rough edge measured in the shipped 1.2.1 build gets finished, Chat first.
The list lives in the plan and grows as building surfaces more. None of it is
a style change; each is copy, a state, or noise.

### 7. Vendor names

They stay where they are, one size smaller, and collapse into a small "ai"
glyph that expands on hover. One component, reused wherever a vendor shows.
The jargon test bans HTTP status codes, raw model identifiers and internal
identifiers from the face; it does not ban vendor names.

### 8. Tuning

A new surface where the user tunes everything Grux does for them, organized
by what Grux does rather than by subsystem. Its shape is decided from visuals
before it is built.

### 9. First run

Opens as one centered prompt: "What do you want to do with Grux?" The answer
drives a dynamic onboarding that tailors the setup without random permission
prompts. Listening is named on the first screen with its off state explained.
The command palette and the Developer and Labs doors are named.

## What does not change

Design tokens, palette, radii, motion, typography. The phone companion. The
CLI. Both bundle identifiers. The Foundry engine internals. No telemetry.

## How we know

- A test fails on HTTP codes, raw model ids or internal identifiers in the face.
- A test asserts at most 14 sidebar rows at first run and no per-row BETA pill.
- A test asserts every registry row has a door reachable from onboarding or
  Settings.
- A test plants a 0.99-confidence destructive command and asserts it stops.
- Switching the Listening control to After "Hey Grux" flips `mic-status.json`
  to wake on, ambient off, and back.
- The open source build carries no decision-provider key; the key is only ever
  read from the user's Keychain.
- Build, tests (count not below 3.0's predecessor), contract check and the OSS
  guarantee all pass.
- Chat matches the accepted finished face, captured from the running app.
- Release notes carry the measured before and after latency of every decision
  point moved to the engine.
- A stranger on a clean macOS VM says "open my calendar" with no wake word and
  it opens in under a second.
